# OKRs Source Notes

> Skill: `okrs`
> Retrieved: 2026-05-30
> Purpose: local grounding notes for Objectives and Key Results as a goal-setting and strategy-execution methodology.

## Primary Sources

- What Matters, "What is an OKR? OKR Meaning, Definition & Examples"
  - URL: https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/okr-meaning-definition-example
  - Evidence used: OKRs combine an Objective with supporting Key Results. Objectives state what is to be achieved; Key Results monitor progress toward the Objective.
  - Evidence used: Effective Key Results are specific, time-bound, aggressive but realistic, measurable, and verifiable. Typical OKR shape is one Objective with 3-5 Key Results.
  - Evidence used: What Matters attributes OKRs to Andy Grove at Intel, taught to John Doerr, then popularized through Google.

- Google re:Work, "Set goals with OKRs"
  - URL: https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs
  - Evidence used: Google describes OKRs as ambitious, public, measurable, and easy to grade. It separates OKRs from employee evaluations and from shared to-do lists.
  - Evidence used: The guide recommends a limited number of Objectives, roughly three Key Results per Objective, outcome-based Key Results rather than activity lists, and regular grading/check-ins.
  - Evidence used: Google treats the 0.6-0.7 scoring range as a healthy stretch-goal signal in its aspirational use of OKRs.

- OKR Institute, "What Are OKRs? The Complete Guide to Objectives and Key Results"
  - URL: https://okrinstitute.org/okrs/
  - Evidence used: Supplemental confirmation that OKRs pair qualitative Objectives with quantitative Key Results and are used for measurable, outcome-driven priorities.
  - Evidence caveat: This is a practitioner/training organization, not the original source. The skill treats What Matters and Google re:Work as stronger sources.

## Stable Framework Facts Used by the Skill

- OKRs are a strategy-execution and goal-setting method, not the upstream strategy itself.
- Objectives describe meaningful outcome states for a period.
- Key Results define measurable evidence that the Objective has been achieved.
- Strong Key Results include a baseline, target, evidence source, and review date.
- OKRs should be few enough to force focus and trade-offs.
- OKRs are public or at least visible enough to create alignment.
- OKRs are reviewed and scored; the score should produce learning, not compensation negotiation.
- Aspirational OKRs and committed OKRs require different scoring expectations.

## Failure Modes to Preserve

- Writing Objectives as vague aspirations without strategy context.
- Writing Key Results as task lists, launches, meetings, or activity volume without outcome evidence.
- Creating too many Objectives and diffusing focus.
- Omitting baselines, targets, dates, or data sources.
- Treating OKRs as KPI dashboards for business-as-usual monitoring.
- Treating OKR grades as direct employee evaluation or compensation inputs.
- Forcing lower-level teams to mirror every higher-level OKR instead of aligning to the relevant priority.
- Writing OKRs once and never checking in, grading, or learning.
