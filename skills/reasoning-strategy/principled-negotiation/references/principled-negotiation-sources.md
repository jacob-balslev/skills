## Primary Sources

- Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School, "Principled Negotiation: Focus on Interests to Create Value."
  - URL: `https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/principled-negotiation-focus-interests-create-value/`
  - Used for the four elements of principled negotiation: separate people from the problem, focus on interests not positions, invent options for mutual gain, and insist on objective criteria. Also used for the BATNA reminder and agreement-better-than-BATNA framing.
- Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School, "What is BATNA? How to Find Your Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement."
  - URL: `https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/batna/translate-your-batna-to-the-current-deal/`
  - Used for the BATNA definition and the implications for accepting proposals, negotiation leverage, and walking away.
- Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School, "Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement: Beyond the Basics."
  - URL: `https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/batna/best-alternative-to-a-negotiated-agreement-beyond-the-basics/`
  - Used for the strong-BATNA-as-power framing and for the operational requirement to determine and improve a backup plan before and during negotiation.
- Fisher, Roger; Ury, William; Patton, Bruce. *Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In.*
  - Referenced through Harvard Program on Negotiation summaries above.
  - Used as the named origin of principled negotiation, negotiation on the merits, and BATNA in this skill.

## Skill Interpretation

This skill paraphrases principled negotiation into an agent-usable workflow. It does not reproduce the book. The operational additions are agent-facing checks:

- build a negotiation map before drafting advice,
- separate people/process issues from substantive deal issues,
- translate positions into interests,
- create multiple option packages before commitment,
- identify objective criteria that both sides can discuss,
- define BATNA, reservation value, target value, and likely ZOPA,
- recommend accept, improve, pause, or walk away based on BATNA comparison.

## Boundary Notes

- Principled negotiation prepares and conducts negotiations with counterparties.
- Expected value compares actions under quantified uncertainty without requiring a counterparty.
- Playing to Win formulates integrated strategy choices that may precede a negotiation.
- Positioning frames a product's differentiated value; it does not structure deal-table bargaining.
- Radical Candor handles feedback conversations; it does not own BATNA, ZOPA, or agreement design.
- This skill is not legal advice, HR advice, safety intervention, or crisis negotiation guidance.
