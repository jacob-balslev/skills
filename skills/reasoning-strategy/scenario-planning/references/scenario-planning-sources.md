- UK Government Office for Science, [Futures toolkit publication page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts). Used to verify publication status, 2024 update, and the toolkit's stated purpose: policies and strategies robust to uncertain futures.
- Paul J. H. Schoemaker, [Scenario Planning: A Tool for Strategic Thinking](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/scenario-planning-a-tool-for-strategic-thinking/), MIT Sloan Management Review, 1995. Used for the management-strategy framing: combating overconfidence and tunnel vision by identifying trends and uncertainties and constructing multiple futures.
- Shell, [Scenarios](https://www.shell.com/what-we-do/energy-and-innovation/scenarios.html). Used as an authoritative practitioner anchor for the Shell scenario-planning tradition and ongoing scenario practice.
- OECD, [Strategic foresight](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/strategic-foresight.html). Used as an institutional anchor for foresight as a public-strategy discipline.
- Matthew Spaniol and Nicholas Rowland, [The scenario planning paradox](https://doi.org/10.1002/ffo2.3), Futures & Foresight Science, 2018. Used for the caution that scenario planning is a method with process/content traps and should not be treated as a mechanical prediction engine.

## Method Commitments Used In The Skill

1. Scenario planning is a decision-support method, not a prediction claim.
2. The focal decision, time horizon, scope, and stakeholders must be explicit before scenario construction.
3. Scenario inputs can come from PESTEL, driver mapping, horizon scanning, stakeholder interviews, market research, policy research, industry analysis, and weak-signal work.
4. Predetermined elements and critical uncertainties must be separated.
5. Scenario axes or logics must be high-impact, uncertain, relevant, plausible, and not redundant.
6. Useful scenarios are plausible, internally consistent, distinct, memorable, and decision-relevant.
7. The common optimistic/base/pessimistic format is weaker than distinct causal worlds unless it is explicitly just a sensitivity analysis.
8. Scenario work is incomplete until it changes choices: robust actions, contingent actions, hedges, options, stops, monitoring triggers, or research priorities.
9. Signposts need observable indicators, source or collection method, cadence, owner, and a linked decision.
10. Scenario planning can feed strategy cascades, roadmaps, OKRs, expected-value calculations, and policy stress-testing; it does not replace those downstream methods.

## Boundaries Against Existing Skills

| Neighbor | Scenario-planning boundary |
| --- | --- |
| `pestel` | PESTEL scans macro forces; scenario planning selects critical uncertainties and turns them into alternative futures. |
| `expected-value` | Expected value compares options when probabilities and payoffs are defensible; scenario planning is for deep uncertainty where fake probabilities would mislead. |
| `bayesian-reasoning` | Bayesian reasoning updates belief in propositions; scenario planning holds multiple plausible worlds for strategy stress-testing. |
| `three-horizons` | Three Horizons balances innovation portfolios over time; scenario planning explores alternative external futures. |
| `swot-tows` | SWOT/TOWS organizes internal/external factors and generates options; scenario planning stress-tests options across future worlds. |
| `playing-to-win` | Playing to Win owns the final strategy choices; scenario planning informs assumptions, robustness, and contingencies. |
