## Authoring Interpretation

The skill treats the BCG Matrix as a portfolio allocation framework, not as a final recommendation engine. The live axes are market growth and relative market share, both relative to a clearly defined market and decision horizon. The operational output should be a classified portfolio with cash-flow hypotheses, evidence gaps, risk and assumption notes, allocation implications, and clear handoff to valuation, competitive analysis, growth-path analysis, or integrated strategy formulation.

Risk and action language is deliberately qualified. Classic BCG logic often implies that cash cows fund stars and selected question marks, while weak low-growth positions may be harvested, divested, repositioned, or exited. The skill does not treat these as universal commands. Modern markets can move quickly, relative share may be a weaker proxy for advantage, and a low-share/low-growth unit may still have strategic, platform, mission, regulatory, or customer-retention reasons to remain in a portfolio.

## Boundary Notes

- Ansoff Matrix owns product-market growth path classification by existing/new products and existing/new markets.
- SWOT/TOWS owns internal/external factor inventory and option generation. BCG can use factors as evidence, but it does not own the factor matrix.
- PESTEL owns macro-environment scanning. BCG can use macro evidence to assess market growth, but it does not produce political/economic/social/technological/environmental/legal analysis.
- Porter's Five Forces owns industry profit-pressure structure. BCG can say a unit is in a high-growth market; Five Forces tests whether that market is structurally attractive.
- Seven Powers owns durable advantage-source classification.
- Playing to Win owns integrated strategy choices.
- Expected Value owns probability-weighted comparison after options, outcomes, probabilities, and values are modeled.
