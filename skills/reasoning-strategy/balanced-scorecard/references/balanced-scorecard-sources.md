# Balanced Scorecard Sources

Created: 2026-06-12T00:04:16Z

## Source Notes

- Robert S. Kaplan and David P. Norton, [The Balanced Scorecard: Measures That Drive Performance](https://hbr.org/2005/07/the-balanced-scorecard-measures-that-drive-performance), Harvard Business Review, 2005 republication of the classic article. Used for the core premise that measurement systems shape behavior and that traditional financial measures alone are insufficient for continuous improvement, innovation, and future capability.
- Robert S. Kaplan and David P. Norton, [Using the Balanced Scorecard as a Strategic Management System](https://hbr.org/2007/07/using-the-balanced-scorecard-as-a-strategic-management-system), Harvard Business Review, 2007 republication. Used for the framing of the Balanced Scorecard as a complement to financial measures and a strategic management system linking current actions to longer-term goals.
- Balanced Scorecard Institute, [Balanced Scorecard Basics](https://balancedscorecard.org/bsc-basics-overview/). Used for the practical implementation sequence: communicate strategy, align daily work, prioritize projects/products/services, measure progress, use the four perspectives, strategy maps, KPIs, strategic initiatives, and cascading.
- Bain & Company, [Balanced Scorecard - Management Tools](https://www.bain.com/insights/management-tools-balanced-scorecard/), published 2023-01-31. Used for the management-tool definition, implementation steps, example performance categories, and common uses such as linking objectives to budgets, tracking strategy elements, resource allocation, organizational change, and execution coordination.

## Method Commitments Used In The Skill

1. A Balanced Scorecard starts from strategy, mission, vision, or strategic theme. It is not a metric-inventory exercise.
2. The scorecard balances financial or stewardship outcomes with customer/stakeholder outcomes, internal process performance, and learning/growth or organizational capacity.
3. Perspective labels may be adapted for public-sector, nonprofit, product, or transformation work when the balancing mechanism remains intact.
4. Strategic objectives must be distinct from measures and initiatives.
5. A strategy map is a cause-effect hypothesis, not decorative arrows.
6. Measures need definitions, baselines, targets, owners, data sources, cadence, and interpretation rules.
7. Strategic initiatives should be linked to the objectives and measures they are expected to move.
8. Cascading means translating strategy to a lower level, not copying every parent metric.
9. Review cadence must produce decisions and learning, not only red/yellow/green reporting.
10. A scorecard should complement financial measures, not replace them or let them dominate.
