## Skill Interpretation

This skill turns the Kano model into an agent-usable product and feature-prioritization method. It does not reproduce the source papers. The operational additions are:

- define the customer segment and decision context before classifying features,
- phrase each feature as a user-visible capability or outcome,
- ask paired functional and dysfunctional questions rather than simple "do you want this?" questions,
- classify responses into must-be, performance, attractive, indifferent, reverse, or questionable categories,
- treat categories as segment-specific and time-sensitive,
- prioritize must-be gaps first, performance trade-offs by evidence and cost, and attractive features as differentiators only after basics are covered.

## Boundary Notes

- Kano classification explains how customer satisfaction responds to the presence or absence of a feature.
- General prioritization frameworks such as RICE, ICE, MoSCoW, and expected value rank work by score, confidence, effort, payoff, or release necessity.
- User research finds and synthesizes customer evidence; Kano is one structured way to classify feature-response evidence.
- Positioning chooses market context and differentiated value; Kano can supply evidence about what buyers perceive as basic, performance-driving, or surprising.
- The method is not a substitute for accessibility, safety, legal compliance, reliability, or other non-negotiable requirements.
