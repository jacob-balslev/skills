# PESTEL Sources

Reviewed 2026-06-03.

## Concept Sources

- CIPD, "PESTLE analysis", factsheet dated 2026-05-20.
  - URL: https://www.cipd.org/uk/knowledge/factsheets/pestle-analysis-factsheet/
  - Used for: PESTLE as a scan of external political, economic, sociological/social, technological, legal, and environmental factors; the operational steps of scoping, sourcing, analyzing, rating importance, deciding actions, and monitoring; advantages and common limitations such as oversimplification, too much data, assumptions, pace of change, and need for regular updates.
- Oregon State University Open Educational Resource, "The General Environment (PESTEL)" in Strategic Management.
  - URL: https://open.oregonstate.education/strategicmanagement/chapter/3-the-general-environment-pestel/

- Oregon State University Open Educational Resource, "Strategy Analysis Framework (SAF)" in Strategic Management 2E.
  - URL: https://open.oregonstate.education/strategicmanagement2e/chapter/8-strategy-analysis-framework-saf/

- Open Library bibliographic record for Francis J. Aguilar, "Scanning the Business Environment" (1967).
  - URL: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL5535031M
  - Used for: historical grounding of business-environment scanning; not used as the operational procedure because the live skill is grounded in accessible current PESTEL/PESTLE references.

## Authoring Interpretation

The skill treats PESTEL/PESTLE as a broad external-environment scan, not as a strategy-choice method. The method should produce evidence-backed signals, implications, assumptions, and monitoring triggers that can feed downstream strategy tools.

The six categories are prompts, not proof of completeness. A strong PESTEL output must state geography, market or segment, time horizon, evidence quality, impact, likelihood, uncertainty, and the next method when the table is no longer enough.

## Boundary Notes

- SWOT/TOWS owns internal/external factor inventory and option generation. PESTEL can supply external opportunities and threats but does not own strengths, weaknesses, or TOWS crossing.
- Porter's Five Forces owns industry profit-pressure structure. PESTEL can identify macro forces that affect the industry, but buyer power, supplier power, entrants, substitutes, and rivalry belong to Five Forces.
- Blue Ocean Strategy owns value-curve reconstruction and new-demand design.
- Playing to Win owns integrated strategy choices.
- Expected Value owns probability-weighted option comparison.
