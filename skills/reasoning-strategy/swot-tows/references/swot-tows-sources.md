# SWOT/TOWS Sources

Reviewed 2026-06-08.

## Source Set

- Weihrich, Heinz. "The TOWS Matrix - A Tool for Situational Analysis." *Long Range Planning* 15(2), 54-66, April 1982.
  - Bibliographic record: `https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1361137044448123776`
  - DOI: `10.1016/0024-6301(82)90120-0`
  - Used for the TOWS matrix as an action-oriented situational-analysis tool and for SO/WO/ST/WT strategy option logic.
- Australian Government business.gov.au. "Do a SWOT analysis."
  - URL: `https://business.gov.au/planning/business-plans/do-a-swot-analysis`
  - Used for a public business-planning summary of SWOT as strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats; internal strengths/weaknesses; external opportunities/threats; and the practice of using SWOT to develop strategies.
  - Rechecked 2026-06-08: the page still defines SWOT as a strategic planning tool and separates strengths/weaknesses as internal from opportunities/threats as external.
- Watkins, Michael D. "From SWOT to TOWS: Answering a Reader's Strategy Question." Harvard Business Review, March 27, 2007.
  - URL: `https://hbr.org/2007/03/from-swot-to-tows-answering-a-readers-strategy-question`
  - Used for the practical critique that SWOT is often done poorly and that TOWS is the move from factor listing to strategy options.
  - Rechecked 2026-06-08: the HBR article remains available at the same URL.

## Skill Interpretation

This skill turns SWOT/TOWS into an agent-usable strategy method. It does not reproduce source templates. The operational additions are:

- frame the decision and time horizon before creating quadrants,
- classify factors by internal/external and helpful/harmful axes,
- require evidence or mark assumptions,
- prioritize decision-relevant factors before crossing them,
- generate SO, WO, ST, and WT strategic options,
- identify what method or evidence should follow.

## Boundary Notes

- SWOT/TOWS inventories and crosses situational factors into options.
- Porter's Five Forces analyzes industry structure and profit pressure.
- Seven Powers classifies durable advantage sources.
- Blue Ocean Strategy reconstructs value curves and market boundaries.
- Playing to Win integrates strategy into five linked choices.
- OKRs translate chosen strategy into objectives and key results.
- Expected value compares quantified outcomes with probabilities and values.
