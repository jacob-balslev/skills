# STP Marketing Sources

Reviewed 2026-06-10T00:02Z.

## Concept Sources

- University of Minnesota Libraries, "5.2 How Markets Are Segmented" in *Principles of Marketing*.
  - URL: https://open.lib.umn.edu/principlesmarketing/chapter/5-2-how-markets-are-segmented/
  - Used for: market segmentation bases, especially behavioral, demographic, geographic, and psychographic bases; the need to classify buyers with variables that create real customer value; and the difference between consumer and B2B segmentation.
- University of Minnesota Libraries, "5.3 Selecting Target Markets and Target-Market Strategies" in *Principles of Marketing*.
  - URL: https://open.lib.umn.edu/principlesmarketing/chapter/5-3-selecting-target-markets-and-target-market-strategies/
  - Used for: target-market attractiveness criteria: size/profitability, growth, competitive crowding or distinctiveness, accessibility, organizational resources, and mission/objective fit; plus target-market strategy choices such as mass, multisegment, concentrated, and micromarket approaches.
- University of Minnesota Libraries, "5.4 Positioning and Repositioning Offerings" in *Principles of Marketing*.
  - URL: https://open.lib.umn.edu/principlesmarketing/chapter/5-4-positioning-and-repositioning-offerings/
  - Used for: positioning as customer perception relative to competition, perceptual maps, and repositioning when the desired market perception or target segment changes.
- Harvard Business Review, Shapiro and Bonoma, "How to Segment Industrial Markets" (1984).
  - URL: https://hbr.org/1984/05/how-to-segment-industrial-markets
  - Used for: B2B segmentation needing nested firmographic, operating, purchasing, situational, and personal-characteristic lenses rather than consumer-only demographic/psychographic bases.
- Wendell R. Smith, "Product Differentiation and Market Segmentation as Alternative Marketing Strategies", *Journal of Marketing* (1956).
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/1247695
  - Used for: historical grounding of market segmentation as a marketing strategy concept; not used as the operational procedure because the live skill uses accessible current teaching sources.
- Dickson and Ginter, "Market Segmentation, Product Differentiation, and Marketing Strategy", *Journal of Marketing* (1987).
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/002224298705100201
  - Used for: the distinction between market segmentation, product differentiation, and marketing strategy; especially useful for keeping STP separate from standalone positioning or product-differentiation work.

## Authoring Interpretation

The skill treats STP as a connected sequence, not three independent headings. Segmentation produces evidence-backed candidate groups; targeting chooses where to focus using attractiveness and fit; positioning translates the chosen target into a differentiated market meaning and downstream marketing implications.

The skill also treats B2B, nonprofit, and product-led contexts as legitimate STP contexts. It does not force consumer-demographic segmentation when firmographic, needs-based, usage, maturity, account, buying-center, or mission-fit variables explain the decision better.

## Boundary Notes

- `positioning` owns standalone product positioning, category choice, competitive alternatives, and value-theme framing when the target and competitive context are already chosen.
- `pestel` owns external macro-environment scanning; STP may use PESTEL evidence when macro forces affect target attractiveness.
- `porters-five-forces` owns industry profit-pressure structure; STP owns which customer segment to serve and how to position to that segment.
- `blue-ocean-strategy` owns value-curve redesign and new-demand creation; STP owns focus inside a defined market or buyer population.
- Tactical marketing-mix work owns Product, Price, Place, Promotion, People, Process, and Physical-evidence design after the target and position are set.
- Copywriting work owns message structure and wording; STP owns the upstream choice of audience and position that copy should express.
