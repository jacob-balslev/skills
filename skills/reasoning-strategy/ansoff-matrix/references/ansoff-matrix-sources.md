## Authoring Interpretation

The skill treats the Ansoff Matrix as a growth-direction classifier, not a recommendation engine. The two live axes are product newness and market newness relative to the actor's current baseline. The operational output should be a classified option set with assumptions, strategic distance, evidence needs, risk drivers, sequencing logic, and a clear handoff to downstream strategy or valuation methods.

Risk language is deliberately qualified. Diversification usually carries a larger learning burden because both product and market are new, but the skill does not claim universal risk rankings. Actor capabilities, acquisition paths, partnerships, customer adjacency, and staged experiments can change the actual risk profile.

## Boundary Notes

- SWOT/TOWS owns internal/external factor inventory and option generation. Ansoff can classify growth direction after or before factor work, but it does not own the factor matrix.
- PESTEL owns macro-environment scanning. Ansoff can use macro evidence, but it does not produce political/economic/social/technological/environmental/legal analysis.
- Porter's Five Forces owns industry profit-pressure structure. Ansoff can say "new market"; Five Forces tests whether that market is attractive.
- Playing to Win owns integrated strategy choices. Ansoff can feed where-to-play and growth-path thinking, but it does not define the full cascade.
- Seven Powers owns durable advantage-source classification.
- Blue Ocean Strategy owns value-curve reconstruction and demand creation.
- Expected Value owns probability-weighted comparison after options, outcomes, probabilities, and values are modeled.
