# Value Chain Analysis Sources

Reviewed 2026-06-07T00:03Z.

## Concept Sources

- Harvard Business School Institute for Strategy & Competitiveness, "The Value Chain".
  - URL: https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-value-chain.aspx
  - Used for: Porter's value-chain framing; disaggregating a company into strategically relevant activities; sources of competitive advantage as activities that produce higher prices or lower costs; the value system upstream and downstream of the firm; and the idea that strategy is reflected in how activities are configured and linked.
- Harvard Business School Institute for Strategy & Competitiveness, "Strategy Explained".
  - URL: https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/Pages/strategy-explained.aspx
  - Used for: strategy as a unique and valuable position, trade-offs, fit across the value chain, business-unit strategy as the core level in Porter's work, and the relationship between competitive advantage and superior performance.
- Harvard Business School Institute for Strategy & Competitiveness, "Strategic Positioning".
  - URL: https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/strategic-positioning.aspx
  - Used for: strategic positioning as choices about what value to create and how to create it differently than rivals; premium price or lower cost as the performance mechanism; and the role of activity choices in shifting relative price or relative cost.
- Harvard Business School Faculty & Research publication record, Michael E. Porter, `Competitive Advantage: Creating and Sustaining Superior Performance`.
  - URL: https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=191
  - Used for: official publication record for the 1985 source work that introduced the value-chain framework as a tool for diagnosing competitive advantage.

## Authoring Interpretation

The skill treats Value Chain analysis as an activity-system strategy method, not as a generic supply-chain diagram. The operating sequence is:

1. Define the business unit, offering, customer, competitor set, and strategic question.
2. Map primary and support activities at the level where value and cost can be discussed.
3. Attach value, cost, speed, quality, risk, learning, and margin effects to activities.
4. Trace linkages and fit across activities.
5. Compare the activity configuration to rivals, substitutes, or the current baseline.
6. Convert activity-level findings into strategic options, operating changes, or downstream analyses.

The classic primary/support activity labels are retained as a starting checklist. They are not treated as a fixed org chart because the same strategic mechanism appears differently in manufacturing, services, software, marketplaces, healthcare, and nonprofits.

## Boundary Notes

- Porter's Five Forces owns external industry structure and profit-pressure diagnosis.
- VRIO owns resource/capability durability testing through value, rarity, imitation cost, and organization.
- Seven Powers owns durable power-source classification.
- BCG Matrix owns portfolio allocation by market growth and relative market share.
- Ansoff Matrix owns product-market growth path classification.
- SWOT/TOWS owns broad internal/external factor inventory and option generation.
- PESTEL owns macro-environment scanning.
- Expected Value owns quantified probability-weighted option comparison.
