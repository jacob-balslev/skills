# Porter's Five Forces Sources

Reviewed 2026-05-26.

## Primary Sources

- Harvard Business School Institute for Strategy and Competitiveness, "The Five Forces".
  - URL: `https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx`
  - Used for the five force names, the industry-structure/profitability framing, dynamic-structure note, and force-level drivers.
- Harvard Business Review, Michael E. Porter, "The Five Competitive Forces That Shape Strategy", January 2008.
  - URL: `https://hbr.org/2008/01/the-five-competitive-forces-that-shape-strategy`
  - Used for the extended-rivalry framing: competition for profits includes customers, suppliers, potential entrants, substitutes, and established rivals.

## Skill Interpretation

This skill paraphrases the framework into an agent-usable workflow. It does not copy the HBS or HBR text. The operational additions are agent-facing checks:

- define the industry boundary before scoring forces,
- connect each force to structural drivers,
- separate substitutes from direct rivals,
- separate industry structure from firm-specific advantage,
- connect force strength to profitability and value capture,
- assess dynamic trends over the relevant time horizon,
- finish with strategic implications rather than a force-name table.

## Boundary Notes

- Five Forces diagnoses industry structure and profit-pool pressure.
- Playing to Win formulates integrated strategy choices after or alongside that diagnosis.
- Seven Powers classifies durable sources of company power or moat.
- SWOT/TOWS inventories internal and external factors; it does not explain industry profit pressure by itself.
- PESTEL scans macro trends; it does not replace a force-by-force competitive-structure analysis.
- Market sizing estimates demand; it does not determine whether the industry structure lets participants keep profits.
